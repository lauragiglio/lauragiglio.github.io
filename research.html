Research

Here is a summary of my current and previous research projects.

Cross-decoding of word production and comprehension in post-stroke aphasia

Thanks to an SNSF Postdoctoral Fellowship I was recently awarded, I am setting up an experiment to investigate the neural correlates of semantic and phonological processing across word production and comprehension using EEG. In this project, I aim to take advantage of multivariate pattern analysis to perform temporal decoding across production and comprehension in neurotypical speakers and speakers with aphasia.

Verb planning from a cross-linguistic perspective

In several EEG projects, I aim to understand what factors affect verb planning, using languages with different properties to address questions on morphological encoding, lexical retrieval and meaning composition. On-going work is focusing on Chintang, a Sino-Tibetan language spoken in Eastern Nepal (in collaboration with Tribhuvan University, Kathmandu, Nepal), Vietnamese (ULIS-VNU, Hanoi, Vietnam), Mandarin Chinese (thanks to the work of Master's student Runze Liang, now PhD student at the BCBL).

Individual differences in the neural correlates for language processing

In collaboration with Muge Ozker, Peter Hagoort, and the Language in Interaction consortium (Donders Institute, The Netherlands), I am characterizing how brain networks underlying language processing are modulated by individual differences in cognitive skills. We have a first project currently under review (see Ozker et al., bioRxiv), where we show that cognitive skills differently engage neural networks not traditionally associated with language processing during task processing. In a parallel project, I am investigating this specifically during naturalistic listening, to complement previous task work. I recently presented posters on this at SNL and OHBM, and I gave a talk at the meeting of the Italian Association for Cognitive Science.
